## Summary

Opus 5 OAuth fallback single-pass planning audit of docs/security/supply-chain-policy.md and docs/research/source-catalog/adoption-ledger.json. The policy is a genuinely strong draft: dispositions are enumerated, production entry is restricted to `retain`/accepted `adopt`, the GSAP containment rule is explicit and narrow, and the ledger keeps every `unselected`-identity source at codeUseAllowed:false with a named stop gate, kill switch, and cannotOwn list that preserves Page IR, candidate, approval, and release separation. Visual-authoring expansion is correctly NOT authorized: Puck stays evaluate/no-code, GSAP carries expansionStatus blocked plus an explicit motion-builder prohibition, and R3F is blocked. That said, the packet cannot yet carry a safe implementation ticket. One P0: `adapt` is a production-reaching disposition with no required ledger entry, no named reviewer, no written clean-room record, and no CI-detectable artifact, while thirteen shortlist rows already hold adapt/learn dispositions with zero ledger coverage. Seven P1s follow: ledger entries omit most of the policy's own nine mandatory fields yet four assert codeUseAllowed:true, contradicting the policy's explicit forcing rule; there is no machine-readable ledger schema or field vocabulary, so the required CI check "reject invalid state combinations" is unimplementable and fails open on absent fields; GSAP is simultaneously codeUseAllowed:true and releaseUseAllowed:false with no defined build-versus-release or generated-site redistribution boundary; no outbound distribution model is declared anywhere, which makes every license-compatibility determination undecidable; hosted third-party services and subprocessors (OpenRouter and the upstream model providers behind SC-NPM-002) are an entirely missing supply-chain class; "build actions" are in the policy's applies-to scope but have no controls at all; and the ledger's declared planningDispositionAuthority does not cover four of its twelve entries. Blocked, evaluate, research, and license-review states are correctly walled out of production builds by the disposition rule plus codeUseAllowed:false, so containment on those classes holds. Model review is input only; nothing here substitutes for deterministic evidence, named humans, or independent verification.

## Accepted strengths

- Production entry is restricted by an explicit rule — only `retain` and an accepted `adopt` may enter a production build — and every research, evaluate, adapt, needs-license-review, and blocked entry in the ledger carries codeUseAllowed:false, so those five states are correctly walled out of production builds.
- Bounded GSAP use does not authorize visual-authoring expansion: SC-NPM-004 sets expansionStatus blocked, releaseUseAllowed false, cannotOwn includes 'visual motion authoring expansion' and 'human visual approval', and the stop gate names motion-builder and expanded visual authoring as prohibited without written scope consent.
- The pre-policy retain containment rule is written narrowly, names GSAP 3.15.0 as its only current application, and states outright that it is not a general grandfathering path — an unusually disciplined way to handle an inherited license ambiguity.
- licenseReviewerRole for GSAP explicitly records that no person is named and no acceptance is inferred, which resists the common drift of treating a role reference as an approval.
- Every ledger entry carries a cannotOwn list that consistently preserves separation of Page IR and site-document authority, candidate identity, human approval, membership, qualification, and release — including for LiveKit (invitation, candidate lock, membership) and screenshot-to-code (HTML source, candidate, release).
- The policy states that the lockfile, adoption ledger, and SBOM are three distinct inventories and none substitutes for the others, which is the correct framing and is frequently gotten wrong.
- Puck is held at evaluate behind synthetic Page IR fixtures with a stop gate requiring proof of no alternate document or persistence authority, so the canvas-reference path cannot become a second document authority.
- The models and datasets section correctly treats community conversions as independent supply-chain items requiring their own publisher, revision, license analysis, and hashes rather than inheriting the base model's license.
- trust_remote_code, custom operators, pickled artifacts, and conversion scripts are confined to an isolated research worker until approved — the right default for the highest-risk executable class in the model path.
- No model, plugin, or skill may hold approval, membership, release, deployment-credential, or risk-acceptance authority, and model quality explicitly never grants tool, mutation, approval, or release authority.
- Vulnerability and license tool failures must report BLOCKED rather than PASS, which closes the standard fail-open hole in scanner-based gates.
- Tool selection is deliberately left to the owning ticket with coverage, false-positive handling, egress, version, and replacement path recorded first — an honest deferral rather than a fabricated toolchain commitment.
- Exceptions require an expiry, an independent reviewer, and a removal ticket, with no permanent exception and no model-authored acceptance valid.
- The incident procedure correctly states that restoring service does not close the incident or waive provenance repair.
- Packaged-artifact controls name concrete forbidden contents (repository history, sites/, .one-box/, secrets, browser profiles, audit scratch, developer home paths), which is specific enough to become a real check.
- The policy holds the line that no retained desktop or remote-content browser package is allowed before the rewritten EB-001 through EB-021 plan passes, consistent with the authority manifest's `rejected` classification for the embedded browser domain.
