## Summary

Opus 5 OAuth fallback adversarial planning audit (Grok 4.6 timed out; this is NOT Grok evidence and does not satisfy any mandatory-Grok workflow rule). Verdict: REVISE. The packet is directionally strong and, importantly, contains no P0: it does not authorize implementation, select a provider, invent numeric thresholds, merge appointment authority into website release, or claim a mechanical pass from model review. Result vocabulary (PASS/FAIL/BLOCKED/NOT_RUN), evidence binding, baseline-before-threshold discipline, and Page IR inheritance-not-duplication are genuinely well constructed. However, the three target files do not yet deliver the traceability they promise. (1) manifest.json's schema omits the exact fields the strategy's own evaluation contract declares mandatory before a ticket is `ready` (evaluation owner, fixture/environment, evidence path and retention class, policy/toolchain binding, expiry, human-vs-deterministic oracle type), so PROG-EVAL-TEST-001 cannot validate what it claims to validate. (2) Four strategy families — CANVAS, CLIENT, QUAL, and the standalone APPT/OPS scope — have no manifest evaluation ID, tier, ticket, or blocking flag, leaving Canvas designer workflow, client review/collaboration, and agency-grade release quality (accessibility, SEO, performance, security headers, visual) without an owner even though the supplied compatibility matrix makes them Release 1 exit blockers. (3) traceability.md assigns OBX-P160 (EOS-005 desktop/browser isolation), OBX-P185 (EOS-008 ExperienceModule boundary) and OBX-P170 (EOS-007) to tickets that no manifest evaluation carries, so 3 of 19 gaps are traceable only on paper. (4) PROG-EVAL-SEC-001 is a meta-oracle ("every applicable threat domain has an owner, fixture, negative test") covering six EOS gaps under one ticket — existence, not a pass/fail condition. (5) PROG-EVAL-UX-001 is blocking:true with no pass criteria, contradicting the packet's own "a command name is not an evaluation" rule. (6) "T1 is merge-blocking" is stated as fact while supplied reviewer-roles evidence records that the live ruleset requires zero approving reviews and CODEOWNERS is deferred. Secondary contradictions: T4/T5 tiers have zero bound evaluations while T3 absorbs drills; SC-001 puts SBOM/vulnerability response in the credential-free T1 tier the strategy assigns to T2/T3; retention rules (90-day raw model artifacts vs product-lifetime repository history) have no named store and no deletion mechanism; flake handling has no detection, retry, or quarantine-registry mechanism; the mandatory-Grok review rule has no fallback clause, which this very audit exercises. Fixes are bounded and documentation-level; no implementation, schema change, provider, invitation, or appointment authorization is granted or implied.

## Accepted strengths

- Result vocabulary is genuinely well specified: PASS/FAIL/BLOCKED/NOT_RUN with explicit "this is not a pass" language for BLOCKED and NOT_RUN, mirrored exactly in manifest.json `resultStates`.
- Baseline-before-threshold discipline is held consistently and correctly: PROG-EVAL-QUAL prose, the non-functional measurement protocol, the UX study, and the supplied compatibility matrix all refuse to invent numeric accessibility, SEO, performance, or usability targets, and require fixture/metric/percentile/sample/tolerance to be frozen before implementation claims success.
- Evidence binding is stated at the right granularity — commit, source authority, candidate/release identity, fixture/manifest version, toolchain, environment, and policy version — with an explicit no-inheritance rule across changed bytes or changed policy.
- Model authority is correctly bounded: models may challenge a packet and explain failures but cannot manufacture a mechanical pass, approve visual quality, accept risk, or authorize release; the traceability closure rule repeats that a model review alone never closes a gap.
- The Page IR manifest is inherited rather than duplicated, satisfying the no-duplication requirement, and existing Page IR thresholds are preserved rather than restated.
- Appointment independence is stated as a one-way rule ("Website success never supplies appointment acceptance"), and the compatibility matrix keeps appointment fields, CTA leases, and schema out of Release 1 source and bundle.
- Named-human visual review is well specified: fixed 1440x900 / 768x1024 / 390x844 captures plus a live keyboard/screen-reader-capable preview, an enumerated rubric, and a receipt carrying reviewer identity, timestamp, candidate hash, rubric version, notes, and decision.
- The non-functional measurement protocol (seven required records) and the prohibition on comparing results collected under different fixed conditions as one trend are strong and directly usable.
- Re-delivery is correctly treated as a new release candidate with no inherited evidence, closing the common "small content change" loophole.
- The tier table's duration values are explicitly run-time targets with a stated split-or-optimize rule rather than permission to skip coverage.
- reviewer-roles.md is honest about external enforcement: it records that the inspected live ruleset had zero required approving reviews and explicitly declines to pretend the target state is already enforced.
- The manifest's requirement arrays and traceability's per-gap table cover all 19 EOS gaps at least nominally, and the closure rule (owner-accepted design, tickets done, blocking evals PASS on current evidence, no unaccepted P0/P1, authority manifest updated) is a defensible bar.
