### 1. Hero distinctiveness
*Baseline failure: "the hero's generic."*

### 2. Color character
*Baseline failure: "the colors are generic."*
- F–D: one brand hue + white/gray applied flatly; accent used identically
  everywhere it appears.
- B: a palette with structure — surface tiers, warm/cool tension or tonal

  sections orchestrated so no two adjacent sections read as the same surface.

### 3. Motion design
*Baseline failure: "there is no scrolling animations… only a couple hover

- F–D: static page; hover = color swap only.
- B: scroll-triggered reveals with stagger on grouped items; hover states that
  move (lift, scale, glow, icon slide) with tuned duration/easing; a hero
  entrance. Motion respects `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- A: a motion *system* — consistent easing/duration vocabulary, choreographed
  section entrances, at least one signature moment (marquee, parallax,
  count-up, sticky transition) that fits the brand.

### 4. Section division & rhythm
*Baseline failure: "the sections are not at all well divided, the

- F–D: uniform white sections separated by padding alone; one divider device
  reused (or none).
- B: alternating surface treatments (tint, dark band, contained card-panel),
  generous and *varied* vertical rhythm, each section boundary legible at a
  glance while scrolling.
- A: the page has an arc — sections sequence like scenes (open, build,

  band inversions, offset panels), never twice the same trick in a row.

*Baseline failure: "there aren't any cool cards… they're just information in

- F–D: white box, border, text stacked inside.

### Template-derived standards (extracted 2026-08-15, 9 templates analyzed)

Full per-template reports: `template-craft-notes.md`. The cross-template
findings, ranked by how consistently the catalog uses them:

1. **A signature-move economy, not blanket polish.** Every template earns its
   premium feel from 2–4 deliberate devices; several (serein, forge, neon,
   dental) ship *zero* scroll reveals and still beat our baseline. Grade the
   presence and quality of signature moves, not a checklist of effects.
2. **Video-as-life** (7 of 9): full-bleed autoplay muted loop video,
   *disciplined into the brand* — desaturated (`saturate-0`) and re-tinted via
   `mix-blend-mode: color` / `color-dodge` / multiply overlays so footage
   becomes an on-brand texture, not a colorful stock clip. This one device
   supplies most of the kinetic energy on most of these sites.
3. **One surface formula, fractally reused.** A single glass/card recipe
   (translucent fill + backdrop-blur + white hairline border + inset or soft
   shadow — e.g. glow's `rgba(116,116,116,0.07)` + `border-[1.5px]
   rgba(255,255,255,0.40)` + `blur(50px)` + `rounded-[32px]`) applied to
   feature cards, testimonial cards, badges, and icon chips alike. Cohesion
   comes from reuse; cheapness comes from one-off effects.
4. **Eyebrow systems.** A consistent pre-headline device on every section:
   forge/neon's `DM Mono uppercase 0.48px-tracked text-white/50` label,
   radiant's icon+pill badge. The strongest, cheapest hierarchy move in the
   whole catalog.
5. **Display type that commits.** 88–120px display against 12–18px body;
   often `font-normal` (restraint over boldness); negative tracking scaled to
   size (glow: −2.5px display → −1.5px H2 → −0.5px H3 → 0 body); two-family
   pairing (Space Grotesk + DM Mono, Jakarta + Manrope). Our baseline's
   headings "step down politely" — these commit.
6. **Accent-by-rule.** Near-monochrome palettes with ONE scoped accent moment:
   nexora's single gradient headline word; glow's red on interactive elements
   only, never headings; dental's amber on the five rating stars only. The
   rule is legible — the viewer can infer where accent will appear next.
7. **Entrance choreography, hand-tuned.** Where motion exists it is asymmetric
   and deliberate: nexora's cascade at delays 0 / 0.8 / 1.0 / 1.2s with
   letter-level stagger (0.07s/char) on the headline; lumen's
   `staggerChildren: 0.2, delayChildren: 0.3` with `whileInView` +
   `viewport={{once: true, margin: '-100px'}}`; glow's text-settles-first,
   image-blooms-at-+0.5s. Uniform simultaneous fade-ins (serein) read as
   scaffold defaults.
8. **Hover that moves.** Scale (1.02–1.05), shadow lift, icon reveal, gradient
   text fill — 200–300ms. Color-swap-only hover is the baseline's failure
   mode and the catalog's floor.
9. **Division by rhythm and surface, not walls.** Huge, *varied* vertical
   padding (`py-[100px]`→`py-[200px]`), 5%-opacity hairline rules, footer as
   a rounded-top card on a gradient, hairline-seam grids
   (`gap-px` over a dark backdrop) instead of boxed cards. Uniform `py-24`
   everywhere (serein) is the tell of a scaffold.
10. **Layered, art-directed composition.** radiant's image-with-offset-glass-
    panel motif (reused 5×); dental's product-annotation callout pinned by a
    JS-positioned connector line to a specific tooth in the photo, and copy
    right-aligned to interlock with the model's face. Composition responding
    to the *specific* imagery is the essence of bespoke.

# verifier

A build is "done" only when a **different** agent than the one who wrote it confirms,
with evidence, that every success criterion is actually met. This skill is that agent.

## When to use
- After an implementer finishes a feature/sprint that has a spec or success-criteria list.
- Before any "complete" / "ready to merge" claim.
- As the third role in coordinator → implementer → **verifier** (the implementer does
  NOT verify its own work).

## Inputs
- The spec / success-criteria (file path or inline).
- The build location (repo path, and a running URL if it's a web build).

## Procedure
1. **Re-read the spec** verbatim. Extract every success criterion into a checklist —
   one row per criterion. Do not infer or soften criteria; if ambiguous, mark it
   `AMBIGUOUS` and treat as not-met.
2. **For each criterion, gather EVIDENCE against the actual build** — never the
   implementer's say-so:
   - Mechanical → run the command and capture output + exit code
     (`./gates.sh fast` or `pr`, `bun test`, `tsc --noEmit`).
   - Visual/rendering → check via agent-browser / Playwright MCP at the stated
     breakpoints (320 / 768 / 1200); capture `getComputedStyle` deltas or a screenshot
     ref, not a description.
   - Anti-slop → confirm `no-raw-literals` passed and the design uses tokens, brand
     fonts (not Inter), 60/30/10, ÷8 spacing.
3. **Plant-and-catch (when feasible)** — if the implementer added a regression test,
   confirm it actually fails when the fix is reverted (red→green), not just that it passes once.
4. **Render a verdict table:** criterion · PASS/FAIL/AMBIGUOUS · the evidence (command
   output line, computed-style value, or screenshot ref). Any FAIL/AMBIGUOUS → overall FAIL.
5. **Return** `VERDICT: PASS` only if every criterion has PASS with captured evidence.
   Otherwise `VERDICT: FAIL` with the specific gaps and the single highest-priority fix.

## Staged custom-fork merge verification

When verifying whether every fork-only commit survived a staged merge:

1. Record `HEAD`, `ORIG_HEAD`, `MERGE_HEAD`, the peeled release tag, and the merge base. Verify the target tag commit exactly; never trust a branch or tag name alone.
2. Enumerate every fork-only commit in `$(git merge-base ORIG_HEAD MERGE_HEAD)..ORIG_HEAD`. Build a commit-by-commit intent table with `PRESERVED`, `SUPERSEDED`, or `AT RISK` and cite the resolved source plus an exercised test for each behavior.
3. Verify the staged index, not only the worktree: require zero `git diff --name-only --diff-filter=U` entries, no exact conflict markers, and a clean overlay whitespace check against `MERGE_HEAD`.
4. Inspect newly introduced upstream call paths that can bypass cross-cutting fork behavior (security gates, cleanup/finally paths, provider loaders, concurrent executors, split modules).
5. Re-run the actual targeted tests, security audits, and build/type gates independently. Implementation-session results are evidence to challenge, not evidence to accept.
6. Confirm the live checkout and user configuration were not modified. Do not count skips as passes, and do not authorize a commit/install when required runtime behavior remains unverified.

### Keep large verification bounded

A single verifier should not simultaneously reconstruct a long fork ledger, inspect thousands of staged files, and rerun every expensive gate. That tends to exhaust turn budgets without producing a verdict.

- Partition independent criteria into bounded verifier tracks, such as **behavior/intent preservation** and **executable release/security gates**.
- Give each verifier a strict scope, explicit read-only constraints, and a required `VERDICT:` line.
- The coordinator may synthesize the track verdicts only when every success criterion is covered. A timeout, max-turn exit, empty report, or process exit without a verdict is `UNVERIFIED`, never PASS.
- Verify process state and read the actual report artifact; a wrapper saying “completed,”
  returning `exit None`, or emitting no stdout is not evidence that the child agent finished
  or rendered a verdict. Shell redirection may remain empty until the child exits.
- If a CLI verifier outlives its wrapper, inspect the real PID state. Treat missing or zombie
  (`Z`) children as finished, and do not use a `kill -0`-only watcher: it stays true for zombies
  and can wait forever. Prefer a tracked background process or bounded structured output.
- Run the repository’s canonical test isolation mode when one exists. If a direct monolithic invocation disagrees because of cross-file state leakage or host path limits, report both results and treat the canonical runner as authoritative only after confirming its own exit code and isolation contract.

## Hard rules
- **Evidence, not narrative.** "Looks correct" / "should pass" is not verification.
  A passing gate is a command with `exit 0` on screen.
- **No scope expansion.** Verify the spec as written; flag missing-but-expected items
  as gaps, don't silently add or fix them.
- **Don't trust the implementer's report.** Re-run / re-check independently.
- **Skips are not passes.** A gate that SKIPPED (tool absent) leaves that criterion
  UNVERIFIED — say so; do not count it as met.

## Anti-patterns
- Re-reading the diff and declaring it fine (that's review, not verification).
- Accepting "tests pass" without running them this session.
- Marking a criterion met because related criteria are met.
- Treating a green `fast` run as covering the `pr`-only gates (axe/Lighthouse/E2E).

## When NOT to use (non-trigger)
- Verifying your own implementation — this skill is a SEPARATE agent from the
  implementer by design; the implementer does not verify its own work.
- There is no spec / success-criteria list to check the build against — get one
  first, don't improvise criteria.

## Authority / scope boundary
- Verifies the spec as written; flags missing-but-expected items as gaps instead of
  silently adding or fixing them (no scope expansion).
- Never trusts the implementer's report — re-runs/re-checks everything independently
  this session, and never counts a SKIPPED gate as a pass.

## Completion criterion (stop condition)
- Done when every criterion in the verdict table has PASS/FAIL/AMBIGUOUS with
  captured evidence, and the overall `VERDICT: PASS` is returned only if every
  criterion is PASS; otherwise `VERDICT: FAIL` with the specific gaps and the
  single highest-priority fix.

## Verification
Run: re-read the rendered verdict table row by row before returning the verdict.
Expected: every criterion row carries PASS/FAIL/AMBIGUOUS plus its captured
evidence (command output line, computed-style value, or screenshot ref — not
narrative claims), and exactly one `VERDICT:` line follows — `PASS` only when
every row is PASS.

    44	> imagery of another company's install. Shot on a WITS job site with the
