# Recommended site structure — WITS

The brief asks the designer to *"recommend the site structure/pages based on the
business and target audience"* and says domain selection is needed. The first
build delivered a single page and no recommendation. This is that
recommendation. (Ledger `D-007`.)

---

## Recommendation: one page now, five pages when there is content

WITS's conversion is a phone call, the buying cycle is short, and there is
currently **no project evidence, no bios and no photography**. A five-page site
built on that would be four pages of padding.

So: **ship the single page**, and treat the additional pages as unlocked by
content, not by launch date.

### Phase 1 — now (built)

One page, in this order. The order is the argument: establish who this is, prove
scope, remove commercial risk, then ask for the call.

| # | Section | Job |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Masthead + persistent phone | The CTA is never more than one glance away |
| 2 | Hero + company record | Who, where, how long, what sector |
| 3 | Scope of work | The three priorities at weight, the rest indexed |
| 4 | Who this is for | Buyer *situations*, not services — how people actually arrive |
| 5 | The difference | The mechanism behind the adjectives |
| 6 | What you receive | Labelled plant, test results, as-built record, one contact |
| 7 | Before we start | Insurance, licensing, site rules, scope sign-off |
| 8 | How a project runs | Answers "how long?" structurally |
| 9 | The work | What the trade looks like |
| 10 | Who you're dealing with | Personal service needs a person |
| 11 | Coverage | Orlando primary, FL, CO |
| 12 | FAQ | What commercial buyers screen on |
| 13 | Contact | Phone and email, large |

### Phase 2 — unlocked by content

Each page ships **only** when its precondition is met. No stubs.

| Page | Precondition | Why it earns a page |
|---|---|---|
| **Projects** (`/projects`) | ≥3 completed jobs photographed with customer permission, with scope and constraints | The single biggest gap. Commercial buyers want to see a job like theirs. Nothing else on the site substitutes for it. |
| **Structured cabling** (`/structured-cabling`) | Nothing — can be written now | The primary service deserves depth and is the main local-search target |
| **Fiber optic cabling** (`/fiber-optic-cabling`) | Nothing | Second priority service; distinct search intent |
| **IT room design** (`/it-room-design`) | Nothing | Third priority; highest-value enquiries |
| **About** (`/about`) | Owner name, photo, short history | Expands section 10 once there is a person to expand it with |

Service pages are worth building before Projects if search visibility matters
sooner — they are the pages people find, while Projects is the page that closes.

### Phase 3 — only if warranted

- **Service area pages** (`/orlando`, `/winter-park`, …) — only with genuine
  per-area content. Thin duplicated location pages hurt more than they help.
- **Insights / notes** — only if WITS will actually write. An abandoned blog
  dated two years ago costs credibility.

**Not recommended at any phase:** online booking (the brief rules it out), a
customer portal, live chat, or a quote calculator. Structured cabling cannot be
priced without a site walk, and pretending otherwise produces junk leads.

## Domain

No domain exists yet. Priorities: short, spellable over the phone, unambiguous
when read aloud to a general contractor.

**Preferred**

1. `witscabling.com` — pairs the acronym with the trade; survives being said
   aloud; the brand is already "WITS"
2. `wigginsits.com` — full surname, closer to the legal name
3. `witsfl.com` — short, geographic, but weakest if Colorado work grows

**Avoid**

- `wits.com` and anything requiring `wits` alone — a common English word, almost
  certainly taken or expensive
- Hyphens — they do not survive being read aloud
- `.net` / `.biz` — reads as dated for a commercial vendor
- Anything embedding "Orlando" while the brief says Colorado is also served

**Register the `.com` plus a defensive `.net`**, point both at the same site, and
set up email on the domain (`estimates@`) rather than a free mailbox. On a
commercial bid, a Gmail address is a small but real credibility cost.

⚠️ Availability was **not** checked — no domain registrar lookup was run. Confirm
before committing to any of these.

## Measurement

The brief's stated purpose is *"establish trust and encourage commercial
customers to call for an estimate."* That gives exactly one primary metric:

- **Calls initiated from the site** — track `tel:` clicks
- Secondary: `mailto:` clicks; scroll depth to the "Before we start" section,
  which is the commercial-risk objection

Do not optimise for time-on-page or bounce rate. A buyer who lands, reads the
company record, and calls in ninety seconds is the ideal outcome and looks like a
bounce.
